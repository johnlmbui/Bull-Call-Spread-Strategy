This project investigates the bull call spread strategy using both theoretical pricing models and real-world market data. Specifically, it applies the Black-Scholes model and put-call parity to compare theoretical option prices with those observed in the market for Apple Inc. (AAPL). The analysis uncovers consistent discrepancies between theoretical and market prices, evaluates the profitability of bull call spreads across various expiration dates, and examines the extent to which put-call parity holds under actual market conditions. The results are illustrated through comparative line graphs of theoretical versus market call option prices, highlighting key limitations of model assumptions, particularly under longer-dated expirations. These findings emphasize the need for caution when applying classical pricing models in practical, real-life trading scenarios.
